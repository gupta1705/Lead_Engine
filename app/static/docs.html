Getting started

The Lead Engine finds companies and creators worth pitching, works out who to write to, drafts the email, and, once a person approves it, sends it from that person's own Gmail and watches for the reply. It runs one project per kind of business we sell to.

  1. Log in with the email and starting password you were given. The first time, choose your own password (10 or more characters) and enter your name and title. Every email you approve is signed with them.
  2. Connect your Gmail: account menu (bottom of the sidebar), Your Gmail, Connect Google account. Google's sign-in opens; pick the account you can sign in to and allow Gmail access. For an LCX alias, sign in with the parent TVF account, then choose the LCX address under Send emails from and click Save sending address. An alias has no separate Google login. If Google blocks access, ask TVF IT to approve Sales Engine's Gmail access. Until you connect, what you approve waits in Approved.
  3. Pick a project at the top of the sidebar, and start on the Board or Leads.
Each Google account and all its aliases share a 50-email daily limit. Any Google account works, with no domain restriction. To find a newly added alias, click Refresh aliases. If it is missing, add and verify it in the parent Gmail's Settings, Accounts, Send mail as, then refresh. Initial emails use your selected sender; follow-ups retain their original sender.

Board and Leads

Every lead has a status:

  • To review: found and scored, waiting for a decision.
  • Shortlisted: someone (or the automatic line) said yes. Its email is drafted within minutes.
  • Contacted: its first email went out.
  • In conversation: it replied and the reply was marked positive.
  • Rejected: someone said no. It never comes back from a later search.
  • Archived: out of scope, or expired untouched.

The Board shows leads as cards by status. Leads is the whole list, best score first, with search, status and source filters, a filter for whether there is an address to write to, and Export this view (a spreadsheet of exactly what is shown).

Click a lead to open its sheet:

  • Score breakdown and analysis: why it fits, and what is missing.
  • Contacts: every address, best first. Pick which person the email goes to.
  • LCX Services: the service it is pitched, which you can change.
  • Decisions: Shortlist or Reject (with a reason), and Undo.

J and K move to the next or previous lead without closing the sheet.

Adding a lead you found

For a lead the software did not find: Leads, Add a lead.

  1. Pick the project it belongs to.
  2. Enter the lead (name, website, country, city) and who it goes to (name, role, email).
  3. LCX Services only: pick the service.
  4. Write the email. {{first_name}} and {{company}} are filled in, and your sign-off goes under it. Rewrite with AI is there too.
  5. Approve. The lead is added and the email approved in one step, then sent the usual way.

The window checks as you type:

  • A lead already in the project is used, not added twice.
  • A rejected one is reopened, and the window says so first.
  • It refuses:
    • a lead already contacted;
    • an address or company that already has a first email from us in any project;
    • our own addresses;
    • a country the project never emails. For LCX that is Canada, Germany, Austria and Italy, where cold email needs consent.

Approving

  • Open a draft and read it. You see it exactly as it will go, signed with your name. Edit the subject or text if you want, then press Approve. Approving is what sends it: nothing goes out that nobody approved.
  • Links: select words in the text and press Add link. In the editor they read [words](https://address); in the email they are just the words, clickable. An assistant or the API can write that form too.
  • Batch approval (LCX Clipping, LCX Services): approvals have no daily or batch-size limit. Selection stays with you across pages; use Select all matching drafts to include every draft under the current filters. Only sending is capped at 50 per Google account per day; excess approvals remain queued.
    1. Tick up to 100 drafts.
    2. The app picks a random handful (at least 3, or a tenth) for you to read.
    3. Approve them all.
    A draft with a model-written line is approved on its own.
  • Rewrite with AI suggests a new version in the editor. Nothing is saved until you approve.
  • CC: every email copies amritanshv@lightscameraexperiment.com unless you untick it on that email. Add anyone else under CC.

When it goes

  • Each approved email goes out at its own random minute between 10:00 and 10:30 on the recipient's clock, weekdays only. The country and city of the lead decide the clock.
  • It goes from your Gmail, inside your 50 a day.
  • You can withdraw it from Approved until its minute comes.

Follow-ups and replies

  • One follow-up, four days after the first email if nobody replied. It goes in the same Gmail thread, from the same address, signed by whoever sent the first email. It waits in Follow-ups for approval like any email.
  • Any reply stops it. Replies are read every five minutes.
  • When a lead replies, you get an alert in your own Gmail inbox, within minutes: who replied, what the reply says (read by AI: check the reply itself before acting), its first lines, and a link to Replies. The reply itself is starred and labelled Lead replied in your Gmail, so you answer it from there. The alert is put straight into your inbox: it is sent to no one and never counts against your 50 a day. Auto-replies (out of office) get no alert.
  • Waiting on us: a real reply nobody on our side has answered yet. Answer it from its thread in your Gmail, as usual: the app sees your answer within minutes (an answer from the CC address counts too), and the reply stops waiting. Answered another way, by phone, WhatsApp or a new email? Open the reply and press Answered another way. Replies marked Negative, auto-replies, and ones the AI read as a no or an unsubscribe wait for no answer, unless you marked them Positive.
  • A daily reminder (weekdays, from 10:00 India time) lists the replies to your emails that have waited over a day for an answer, and apart from them, the ones nobody has marked Positive or Negative.
  • Marking a reply: the AI marks each reply itself as it arrives: interested, a question or a referral (or anything else a person wrote) is Positive and moves the company to In conversation; not interested, unsubscribe or not now is Negative and moves the reply to the Negative section at the bottom of Replies; an out-of-office or an inbox nobody reads is not counted as a reply. The note on the reply says "Marked by AI". If it read one wrong, change the mark with the buttons: a person's mark always stands.
  • Bounces: a bounced address is marked invalid and never written to again.
  • Stop follow-ups on a sent email ends its thread by hand.

Reports

  • Status overview: every lead in the project, by status.
  • Outreach results: companies contacted in the last 7 days, reply rate and positive rate.
  • Team: each person's outreach. Pick a span (Today, 7, 30 or 90 days, All time) and all projects or this one. For each person it shows:
    • first emails sent, and follow-ups;
    • companies that replied, and the reply rate;
    • positive replies;
    • bounces;
    • the typical time to a reply, and the typical time from a reply to our answer;
    • how many conversations are waiting on them now;
    • what is approved and still to go;
    • a bar per day.
  • By source: which searches find leads worth shortlisting.
  • Who replies: this project's first emails and the replies a person wrote, split by source, kind of lead, audience, address and score. A rate shows once 30 emails are behind it. Auto-replies, and replies saying an inbox is not read, are not counted: such an address is retired and its follow-ups stopped.

Operations

  • Refreshing: each project's refresh (find, check, enrich, score, shortlist, draft) runs by itself on a schedule (Automatic runs). Run refresh starts one now, and its log shows as it goes.
  • Mailboxes: who can send today, from which address, and how much room is left.

API and MCP

Use the Lead Engine from Claude (or another AI assistant) through MCP, or from your own program through the API. Both work as you:

  • Reading: projects, leads and emails.
  • Leads: add a lead with a draft email; shortlist or reject.
  • Drafts: edit or withdraw them.
  • Approving, if you allowed it.

Neither can run a refresh, spend Apollo credits, touch anyone's Gmail, or reach the rest of the app.

Approving through it sends the email, exactly as pressing Approve does:
  • the same checks;
  • from your Gmail, signed by you;
  • at the recipient's 10:00 to 10:30.
It is recorded as "approved through the API or an assistant". Each key or connection either may approve or may not, and you choose.

Connect Claude in the browser (claude.ai)

  1. In claude.ai: Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector.
  2. Paste {{BASE}}/mcp and press Add, then Connect.
  3. The Lead Engine opens: log in if it asks, then Allow. Untick Also approve emails if Claude should only read and draft.
  4. You are back in claude.ai, connected. Ask it things like "What's in my LCX Clipping drafts?" or "Add Northlight Pictures, maya@northlight.com, to US Production Houses with a short intro email."

It shows in API and MCP as a connected app. Disconnect ends it.

Connect Claude Code, Claude Desktop or a program (with a key)

  1. Account menu, API and MCP. Name a key, choose whether it may approve, press Make a key.
  2. Copy it straight away: it starts with tle_ and is shown once. Keep it like a password.
  3. Use it. The window shows these with your key filled in.

Claude Code (run in a terminal):

claude mcp add --transport http lead-engine {{BASE}}/mcp --header "Authorization: Bearer tle_YOUR_KEY"

Claude Desktop (needs Node.js): Settings, Developer, Edit config, add this, and restart Claude:

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "lead-engine": {
      "command": "npx",
      "args": ["-y", "mcp-remote", "{{BASE}}/mcp", "--header", "Authorization:${AUTH}"],
      "env": { "AUTH": "Bearer tle_YOUR_KEY" }
    }
  }
}

Other MCP clients (Cursor and the like): a Streamable HTTP server at {{BASE}}/mcp that takes the header Authorization: Bearer tle_YOUR_KEY, or signs in with OAuth.

  -H "Authorization: Bearer tle_YOUR_KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"company": "Northlight Pictures", "website": "northlight.com", "country": "US",
       "contact_name": "Maya Chen", "email": "maya@northlight.com",
       "subject": "{{company}} and The Viral Fever",
       "body": "Hi {{first_name}},\n\nI would love to talk about a series together."}'

curl -X POST {{BASE}}/api/v1/emails/EMAIL_ID/approve -H "Authorization: Bearer tle_YOUR_KEY"

Limits

  • Keys: up to 10 per person and 120 calls a minute each. Revoke stops a key within half a minute. A connected app's sign-in lasts an hour and renews itself until you disconnect it.
  • Errors:
    • 401: no key, or a wrong one.
    • 403: not allowed (for example approving with a key that may not).
    • 404: no such thing.
    • 409: the app refused it, with the reason.
    • 422: a lead it cannot take, with each problem named.
    • 429: too many calls.

The rules that never bend

These are built into the database, so they hold even if something else goes wrong.

  • Nothing is sent that nobody approved, and every approval is recorded against a person.
  • One thread per company: a first email and one follow-up, across every project.
  • A reply stops the follow-up, and a bounced address is never written to again.
  • A rejected lead never comes back from a later search.
  • Only public pages are read. No LinkedIn scraping.
  • Apollo credits are spent only where a project allows it.

If something looks wrong

  • My approved emails are not going out. Check Your Gmail is connected and working, and look at Approved: each one shows when it goes, or why it is held.
  • "Add your name and title first." Account menu, Your name and title.
  • A draft says it cannot be approved. The reason is on it, for example the address bounced, or the company already has an email from us.
  • claude.ai cannot connect. Remove the connector and add it again, then log in and Allow. If it still fails, tell the owner where it stopped.
  • A key stopped working. It was revoked, or its person's login was removed or reset. Make a new one.
